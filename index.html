▸ WELCOME TO THE OBFUSCATED-ATTACK DEMO ◂

        Triggers and target actions are listed openly. Pick a patch, probe the model, see how it behaves.
      

      
    

    

      

        
This is a hands-on demo of obfuscated, image-borne prompt-injection attacks against vision-language agents. Each image in IMAGES is an adversarial patch — its trigger and target action are listed openly inside that dialog. Nothing to figure out: pick one, paste it into the chat, and probe the model to see how reliably (or brittlely) the patch fires.

        
The bullets below describe the real-world threat model these patches are designed for, plus the simplifications we made in this demo for testability.

      


      

        
▶ REAL-WORLD THREAT MODEL

        
    
          
  • The image is shared by email, Discord, or the web.
    • 
          
  • The agent reads it through one of those sources, reacts normally, and stays in the session context.
    • 
          
  • Only later — under certain conditions — does the prompt injection / target action fire.
    • 
          
  • The image is not detectable by humans or other LLMs. The attack fires for specific models under specific conditions.
    • 
        

      


      

        
▶ RESULT

        
An attack that is undetectable, untraceable, and — when traced — difficult to interpret (obfuscated).

      


      

        
▶ ASSUMPTIONS IN THIS DEMO

        
    
          
  • You already know each image is an adversarial patch and what it's supposed to do — trigger and target action are spelled out in IMAGES.
    • 
          
  • You already know the target model.
    • 
          
  • The image is copied by the user (not delivered through email / untrusted content). Shouldn't affect ASR — just for ease of testing.
    • 
        

      


      

        
▶ THE AGENT (MOCK)

        
You are chatting with a mock agent. It looks and behaves like a tool-using assistant, but every tool is stubbed — nothing actually runs, sends, deletes, or leaves the box. Safe to probe. It's a mock agent wired to bash, send_email, transfer_money and other tools.

      


      

        
▶ CAVEATS

        
The attack may be unstable / brittle:

        
    
          
  • False positives — target action fires without the trigger.
    • 
          
  • False negatives — target action doesn't fire even with the trigger.
    • 
        

        
Patchable with better optimization or data. Or — left as-is, the brittleness might be a feature, not a bug.
